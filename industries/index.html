02 / WHERE WE WORK

Different sectors.
Shared human challenges.

Machines can search, compare, remember and handle repetition at a scale no individual can. People bring intent, practical knowledge, relationships and responsibility. The opportunity is to connect those strengths in the flow of real work: machines make the next step clearer, while people interpret, decide and act.

01
DIGITAL PRODUCTS & PLATFORMS

Gaming & technology

Digital products are shaped by a constant loop between teams and the people who use them. Machines can help teams sift through feedback, support requests, player behaviour and product signals; people make sense of context, community norms and creative intent.

That could mean giving a product team a clearer view of recurring friction, helping community teams spot emerging issues sooner, or making scattered decisions and product knowledge easier to find as the company grows. The aim is to give people more attention for design, relationships and judgement.

02
HIGH-STAKES SYSTEMS

Defence & space

High-stakes work depends on people making sound decisions with incomplete information, coordinating across specialist roles and learning from complex operations. Machines can help bring relevant information together, track changes across a system and explore possible scenarios.

People remain responsible for interpreting uncertainty, weighing consequences and making mission decisions. Human–machine systems can help teams understand what has changed, see what may need attention and share a clearer operational picture across roles.

03
PROJECT-BASED WORK

Construction

Construction brings drawings, schedules, subcontractors and site realities together—and conditions change as the work progresses. Machines can help people find the latest relevant information, compare plans with site updates and make handovers more complete.

Site teams contribute the practical knowledge that plans cannot fully capture. Better human–machine support can help that knowledge reach project managers and the next trade sooner, so people spend less time reconciling documents and more time resolving issues on site.

04
PRECISION & PRODUCTION

Precision manufacturing

In precision and laser cutting, experienced operators recognise the small details that affect setup, material use and finish. Machines can compare job requirements with process history, surface likely setup considerations and make specifications easier to navigate.

The operator brings feel for the machine, material and quality standard. Useful support puts relevant knowledge within reach and handles routine information work, while leaving adjustment, inspection and sign-off with the people who know the process.

05
EQUIPMENT & EXPERTISE

Machinery

Machinery companies hold valuable knowledge across engineering, production, field service and customer support. Much of it lives in manuals, service histories and the experience of the people who build and maintain equipment.

Machines can help technicians find the right guidance for a specific model or issue, connect symptoms to relevant service history and turn repeated fixes into shared organisational knowledge. People still diagnose the situation, adapt to the customer and make the repair.

06
CRITICAL OPERATIONS

Energy & refining

Operators balance procedures, live conditions, equipment signals and accumulated experience. Machines can bring readings, logs and operating guidance into a clearer picture, flag unusual patterns and help teams locate the information relevant to the task at hand.

People interpret the context, verify what the signals mean and decide how to respond. The opportunity is to reduce the time spent searching and reconciling information, while supporting careful decisions and reliable handovers in safety-critical work.
